# Agreement is not understanding

The person this set was built for reported the same thing across many runs: a skill decided something on its own, they found out late, and the work went round several times. The cause was written into the skills. Every checklist opened with *"each step opens when the one before it has produced its result"*, and the only places a run stopped for the user were a **gate** — something failed a check — or an ambiguity nobody could resolve from the documents. A blueprint that passed every check was not a failure and not an ambiguity, so `make-exam` wrote it and went straight on to the items, the measurements, the key, the rubric, and the paper before the user had seen the blueprint once. Every artifact after it was rebuilt when the blueprint changed.

Stopping is not enough on its own. The user also said they had told runs to go ahead while not yet knowing what they wanted. A whole draft handed to someone in that state becomes the answer by default, and the "ok" that comes back says only that nobody objected. That is the failure [ADR 0012](0012-a-verdict-is-not-evidence.md) is about, met at the other end of the run: a confirmation is a tick, and a cell that can hold a tick can hold a wrong one.

The 2569 final of a networking course, built by `make-exam`, shows the cost. The paper set before students kept 16 of the 60 items the skill wrote. The blueprint put 40 of them at Apply or above for fourth-year medical-records students, the agent defended the levels as matching the outcomes when the user questioned them, and the user rewrote the paper by hand. The agent was right by the rules it carried — the outcomes said *analyze* and *evaluate*, and alignment asks for the same level — and wrong about the learners, which is something no document in the vault could tell it.

We decided four things.

**A grill point is a stop that happens even when everything passes.** It sits on every step that writes an artifact later work is built on, or one that needs facts only the user holds: the outcomes, the assessment plan, and the teaching plan in `design-course`; the blueprint in `make-exam`; the alignment matrix and the pass standard in `make-practical`; the per-item decisions in `analyze-results`, since dropping an item changes every examinee's mark; and the proposed fixes in `audit-brain`. What it produces is understanding, so it interviews: round by round, each question with a recommendation, the user's answers reshaping what can be asked next. An approval stays where the thing on the table can be judged on its face — a rewritten item beside its measurements in `critique-mcq` — and a grill is used where the user cannot judge a draft until they know what they want.

**Every recommendation carries three answers, and the third is always a question.** Fit — the syllabus line or programme outcome it rests on. Reason — why it beats the alternatives, and what each would cost. Feasibility — whether the people who will carry it out can actually do it, which for anything taught or assessed means this course's equipment, staff, and learners — is asked of the user, never concluded, because fit is what the agent used to answer it on the networking paper. Where the user does not know, the question becomes two or three concrete options with what each leads to; where they still cannot choose, the recommendation is written into the artifact as an open item with a gap marker. Where nobody is there to answer, the run stops at the grill point rather than building on a decision nobody made.

**The grill is a primitive, reached by name.** [ADR 0007](0007-shape-is-shared-process-is-copied.md) says process is copied, and the grill is process. But that rule rests on two reasons from [ADR 0005](0005-duplication-buys-portability.md) — a copied folder must run alone, and the copied text is short — and neither holds here. Every skill that gains a grill point already reaches at least one reference skill by name, so none of them travels alone today; and the procedure with its record format runs close to a thousand words across five carriers, past the size [ADR 0006](0006-how-a-skill-is-reached-and-when-it-is-split.md) found duplicated text stops staying identical. The rule in ADR 0006 that a checklist reaches reference and never another checklist is widened by one kind: a **primitive** has steps of its own but no discovery and no closing check, runs from inside another skill's step, and hands control back when it ends. That is how the interview this set borrows from works in its own home — a multi-step skill there calls its grilling primitive mid-step rather than carrying a copy. Each grill point names `grill-point` in one sentence held identical everywhere, and `_scripts/check.py` holds it so once per grill point, not once per file. A grill writes one line into the artifact it shaped — date, who, how many decisions, which records — and every closing check looks for that line, so a grill that ran can be told from one that was skipped ([ADR 0008](0008-a-check-that-leaves-no-evidence-did-not-run.md)).

**Every checklist shows its plan before it acts.** After the discovery step, the run lists its steps, which of them will stop for the user, and what discovery found or could not find, then waits for the go-ahead; each later step is named by its number as it starts. A run sent to work without the user, as a sub-agent, takes the dispatch as its go-ahead, or an independent critique sent to a sub-agent would stop at its first step. The clause is held identical in every checklist, after the step that first reads the project.

## Considered Options

- **Show a full draft and ask for approval** — rejected: it is what produced the confirmations given without understanding. A draft anchors someone who has no criteria of their own yet.
- **Stop at every file** — rejected: six or seven stops per exam, and frequent confirmations decay into clicking "ok" without reading.
- **Copy the procedure into each skill** — rejected for the reasons above; the copies would protect a portability the carriers do not have.
- **A reference skill holding the procedure** — rejected as a category error: the grill acts, and a reference skill only informs. Naming the new kind keeps the old rule honest instead of stretching it.
- **Let the run continue on the agent's recommendations when nobody answers** — rejected: that is the networking paper exactly, an artifact nobody decided with a whole set built on it.

## Consequences

- `grill-point` is new and model-invoked, since another skill must reach it by name. Its description is written for the calling skill, not for a user asking to be grilled.
- `design-course`, `make-exam`, `make-practical`, `analyze-results`, `audit-brain`, and `init-brain` now need `grill-point` beside them, the last two through `brain-anatomy`, whose twelfth component takes the record format from it; the README says so. A reference skill may point at a shape a primitive holds, as `brain-anatomy` and `learning-outcomes` do.
- `make-practical` splits the pass standard into its own step, so that the grill covers the standard and not the scoring instrument beside it.
- `learning-outcomes` gains the ways out when learners cannot reach an outcome's level, in the order a grill offers them: cut the difficulty that is not the outcome, ladder the levels, or assess below and record the gap.
- Runs are slower and stop more. That cost is the point: the stops land where a wrong answer is rebuilt into everything after it.
- What a grill settles that outlives the run is written down as a decision record — [ADR 0017](0017-the-vault-keeps-its-reasons.md).
