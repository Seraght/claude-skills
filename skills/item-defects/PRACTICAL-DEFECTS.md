# Practical Defects

Defects that practical and performance tasks carry — stations, practical examinations, and any task scored while the examinee works. The defects that apply to items of every kind are in [COMMON-DEFECTS.md](./COMMON-DEFECTS.md), and every task is judged against that file as well as this one.

A practical task is inseparable from three things a written item does not have: its **conditions** (equipment, time, permitted references, safety), its **scoring instrument** (a checklist, a rating scale, or both), and its **markers**. A defect in any of them moves the score as surely as a defect in the task.

**The host project governs.** Where a project or an institution fixes the form, the station count, or the marking scheme, a task is not flawed for following it.

## Group A — marks an unprepared examinee can still earn

- **The product is scored where the performance was meant to be.** The artifact is graded — the drawing, the document, the output — and a better tool, a better template, or better luck produces a better artifact than a better examinee. *Fix:* score what was observed while the examinee worked, or score the process record (the log, the working, the sequence) rather than the finished object.
- **The checklist rewards completion, not competence.** Steps score as done or not done regardless of whether they were done correctly, in a workable order, or for a reason. An examinee who performs every step badly outscores one who skips a step deliberately. *Fix:* anchor each step on the standard it must meet, and score judgement separately on a rating scale.
- **The setup gives the task away.** Equipment laid out in the order of use, a form pre-filled to the point where only one action remains, a single tool on the bench where the choice of tool was the competence being tested. *Fix:* present what a real situation would present, distractor equipment included.
- **Later examinees know the task.** A rotation run in shifts without alternate content lets the first group tell the second. *Fix:* alternate content between sittings, and stagger the order rather than the task where content cannot be alternated.

## Group B — irrelevant difficulty

- **Time and marks out of proportion between tasks.** Where one task costs far more minutes per mark than another, examinees running short abandon the expensive one — and it is usually the highest-level task on the paper, so the exam quietly stops measuring its own top end. *Fix:* compute minutes per mark for every task and bring them into one band, by moving marks or by cutting scope.
- **Parallel forms handed out at random without being equated.** Alternate briefs, cases, or datasets differ in difficulty, and where assignment is random the score carries which form the examinee drew. *Fix:* work each form yourself under exam conditions and compare, then adjust until they match — or give everyone the same form.
- **The task depends on infrastructure the outcome never named.** A live network, an external site being up, a shared printer, a licence server. What fails on the day costs marks for reasons unrelated to the competence. *Fix:* name the dependency in the conditions and provide a documented fallback that earns the same marks.
- **One station measuring several competencies.** A single long task covering three skills produces one score that cannot say which of them failed, and remediation guesses. *Fix:* split into shorter tasks, one competence each.
- **Unequal time or unequal conditions within one rotation**, or a long circuit with no buffer, so late stations are scored on fatigue. *Fix:* one signal for the whole circuit, and a rest point in a long one.

## Group C — congruence, conditions, and correctness

- **The task does not elicit the outcome's verb.** An outcome saying *perform* assessed by a written account of the performance measures writing about it. This is the most common failure in practical assessment and the hardest to see, because the paperwork all mentions the right words. *Fix:* the examinee does the thing, and it is scored while they do it.
- **Conditions unstated.** Equipment, time limit, permitted references and tools, and safety constraints are part of the task, not logistics around it: the same task open-book and closed-book measures different things. *Fix:* state them with the task, in the examinee's copy and in the marker's.
- **No rule for a correct response outside the expected set.** Where a task plants a fixed number of faults, errors, or opportunities, an examinee who finds a genuine one that was not planted is scored as wrong for being right. Real material contains more than was put in it deliberately. *Fix:* state that a defensible response outside the expected set scores in full, and record it for the next revision.
- **The scoring rules are not stated with the task.** All-or-nothing on a sequence, part marks per step, and a critical-error rule that voids the attempt produce very different scores from the same performance. *Fix:* state the rule with the task, and where a safety or critical error voids the attempt, write that into the instrument rather than leaving it to the marker.
- **The examinee's copy misdescribes the material.** The instructions claim the case, the article, or the dataset is one thing and the marker's copy says it is another. Whatever the marking consequence, the task's authenticity — usually its whole reason for existing — is gone. *Fix:* make the two copies agree, and record the provenance in the marker's copy.

## Group D — the examination as a whole

- **No pass standard set before administration.** A cut score decided after the marks are in is decided by the marks. *Fix:* set it beforehand by a stated method, from what a just-competent examinee would be expected to do.
- **Judgement-heavy tasks with one marker and no calibration.** Markers disagree by default; the disagreement lands as marks. *Fix:* calibrate the marking team on shared samples before marking starts, double-mark a sample and the borderline cases, and record the agreement as evidence.
- **Marks concentrated in one station**, so one competence — or one bad ten minutes — carries the outcome. *Fix:* reconcile the marks per station against the blueprint, not against the station count.
- **Per-station results never fed back.** A station nobody fails and a station nobody passes are both telling you something, and neither is visible without looking. *Fix:* record per-station and per-criterion results, and revise from them.
