# Practical Assessment Reference

How a practical examination is built. When the host project carries its own assessment reference, that one governs and this file fills the gaps it leaves. What a defective task looks like is not here — that is `PRACTICAL-DEFECTS.md` in the `item-defects` skill.

## What makes it a practical

The examinee performs, and the performance is scored while it happens. An outcome at *create*, *perform*, or *operate* cannot be evidenced any other way: a written account of a performance measures the account. Everything below follows from that one commitment, which is also what makes a practical expensive — it needs conditions, instruments, markers, and a standard, where a written paper needs a key.

## The alignment matrix

A practical's blueprint is not a grid of topics by cognitive level. It is one row per task:

| Task | What is observed | Criteria | Outcome | Marks | Time |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

- **One task, one competence.** A task covering three skills yields one score that cannot say which failed. Split the work rather than lengthening it.
- **Reliability comes from several independent tasks**, not from one long one — the same reason a longer written paper is more reliable than a short one. Where resources are limited, prefer more short tasks over fewer long ones.
- **Minutes per mark sit in one band across tasks.** Where they do not, examinees short of time abandon the task that costs most per mark, which is usually the highest-level task on the paper.
- **Separate what can be scored objectively from what needs judgement**, task by task. Each hour of judgement-based marking has to be bought with marker calibration; the less of it there is, the more defensible the whole exam becomes. A practical does not need one scoring method throughout.

## Conditions are part of the task

Equipment, time limit, permitted references and tools, permitted use of assistance, and safety constraints. The same task open-book and closed-book measures different things, so they are stated with the task in both the examinee's copy and the marker's. Where the task depends on infrastructure — a network, an external service, a shared device — that dependency is named and given a fallback that earns the same marks.

Where examinees sit in more than one shift, alternate the content between shifts. Where alternate forms exist at all, work each one yourself under exam conditions before deciding they are equivalent: unequated forms handed out at random put the form into the score.

## Checklist or rating scale

Both are legitimate; the task decides which.

| Use a checklist when | Use a rating scale when |
|---|---|
| The procedure has a fixed order and every step matters equally | The work requires adapting to what happens, with no fixed order |
| Steps are unambiguously present or absent | Quality is a judgement an expert makes |
| Markers are not themselves expert in what is being assessed | Markers are expert, and the exam is high-stakes |
| Detailed feedback on what was missed is wanted | An overall standard is what is being judged |

A checklist scored as steps-completed rewards doing everything badly over doing the right things well, so anchor each step on the standard it must meet. Many stations need both: a checklist for the procedural half, a rating scale for the judgement half, and it is normal for the two halves to be scored differently.

## Rubrics

- **Analytic** (separate criteria, each scored) for diagnostic feedback and multi-marker consistency; **holistic** (one overall judgement) for speed on short responses. Analytic is the default when more than one person marks.
- **Criteria are observable.** Each level descriptor names what is present or absent in the work, not how good it felt.
- **Levels are distinguishable.** Adjacent levels differ by something a marker can point at; typically 3–5 levels.
- **Criterion weights sum to the item's marks**, and reflect what the outcome actually values.

## The pass standard

Set it before the examination, by a stated method, from what a just-competent examinee would be expected to do — not from the distribution of marks after the fact, which sets the standard from the cohort rather than from the competence. Where a safety violation or a critical error voids the attempt regardless of the score, that rule belongs in the instrument, stated, rather than in the marker's discretion on the day.

## Markers

Marker disagreement is the default state, and it lands as marks. Before marking begins, the team scores a small shared sample independently and reconciles the differences against the anchors — the step that moves agreement most. During marking, keep one marker per criterion across all examinees rather than one marker per examinee, double-mark a sample and every borderline case, and record the level of agreement as evidence the exam was marked consistently.

## After the examination

Record results per task and per criterion. A task nobody fails and a task nobody passes are both reporting something. Feed both into the next revision, and keep the tasks as a pool so the next examination starts from evidence rather than from scratch.
